Where it does connect to your industrialisation hypothesis is the economy. The post-plague high-wage, land-abundant regime is the context for what Jan de Vries (The Industrious Revolution, 2008) and the "Little Divergence" literature describe: Britain and the Netherlands pulled ahead in real wages from roughly 1500, and that high-wage economy is one plausible precondition for labour-saving innovation. So if you want to argue that reserve enabled industrialisation, the plague gives the argument a material partner: the same shock that entrenched the nuclear, mobile, individualist household also entrenched the high-wage economy. The two are entangled, and disentangling them honestly is a strong chapter rather than a weakness.
