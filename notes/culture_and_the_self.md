Markus and Kitayama's "Culture and the Self" (1991) distinguishes independent from interdependent self-construal. The disclosure model of intimacy is culturally specific to the West; many collectivist societies don't privilege self-disclosure, yet don't show higher loneliness. So "being known" may be an individualist ideal, not a universal need — which is a delicious problem for a paper about Britishness, because it means the British may be lonely by their own cultural standard while a different culture with the same reserve wouldn't register it.
