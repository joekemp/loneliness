Friendship forms through unforced recurrence — the mere-exposure effect (Zajonc), the transformation of weak ties into strong ones (Granovetter), and a brutal time budget: Dunbar's estimate that close friendship costs roughly 200 hours of shared life. Third places (Oldenburg) supplied recurrence without scheduling it. Britain has replaced them with scheduled, paid, transport-requiring, optional encounters — the coffee you arrange, the gym you attend, the meetup you book. Removed is the unforced repetition that has no closing bell. And the policy response — social prescribing, befriending schemes — treats the resulting deficit as an individual failure to be coached, which is the responsibilisation critique we touched on last time.
