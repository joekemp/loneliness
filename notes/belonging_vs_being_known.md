The pub did produce serious things — friendly societies, trade unions, political radicalism, the co-op, Methodist class meetings. So the exception-zones were not empty. But look at what they produced: solidarity, not intimacy. That distinction should become one of your paper's axes. Britain was historically rich in belonging (class, place, nation, club, union) and poor in being known (disclosure of need, fear, love, shame). Loneliness is about being known, not merely belonging — Smith's "mutual sympathy of sentiments," not mere company. You can be lonely inside a dense solidarity; Hoggart's working class is exactly that.
